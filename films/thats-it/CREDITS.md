## Facts on screen

All numbers are Karpathy's own rough estimates ("napkin math"); the film labels them as such and does not draw the tiers to scale.

- About 6 billion people (~75%) have barely come in contact with LLMs.
- About 1–2 billion (~20%) are casual, infrequent users of free-tier chatbots ("derpy chatbots").
- About 20 million (~0.2%) use frontier-grade LLMs professionally in math and code; projects that took weeks or months are completed by agents from a prompt.
- About 5,000 people (~0.00006%) have access to frontier-grade systems inside the labs: swarms of thousands of agents collaborating over weeks, discovering science, advancing mathematics; human review and comprehension are starting to fall behind.
- Three drivers of the funnel: problem size and horizon ("a reservoir of big, difficult problems that you really care about"), jaggedness (capability peaks in digital, verifiable, economically valuable domains, because it comes from reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards), and access (free, paid, internal).
- "This gap in shared understanding of LLM capability is *widening*." (quoted on screen)
- Karpathy's roles, from his own homepage <https://karpathy.ai/>: "I was a research scientist and a founding member at OpenAI" (2015–2017); "I was the Director of AI at Tesla" (2017–2022). Shown on screen as 「OpenAI 创始成员之一 · 前特斯拉 AI 总监」 with the source 「据 karpathy.ai」.
- The line 「不到一年前，他还一个字一个字地敲代码」 is quoted on screen from the post itself: "less than 1 year ago, I was writing code manually by hand, typing memorized computer code commands into a code editor character by character, occasionally pressing Tab…"

The two characters (the woman on the sofa, the programmer next door), the restaurant, and her plan to open her own place are fiction written for the film.
